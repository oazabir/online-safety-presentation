# Teens 10-14 edition: plan (branch `teens-10-14`)

Source deck: 85 slides, written for parents. Target: a 30-minute talk **to 10-14 year-olds**
(25 min talking, 5 min questions), about 24 slides, three audience interactions, no lecturing.

## 1. Principles

| Parent deck | Teen deck |
|---|---|
| "Your child is addicted, you must fix it" | "These apps are built by adults paid to keep you watching. You can beat them." |
| Fear, deaths, court cases | Curiosity, being outsmarted, being in control, being respected |
| Parent controls and blocking | What **you** can do; what to do when something goes wrong |
| Toddler content (Cocomelon, Ms. Rachel), couples, parenting guilt | Gone |
| 3-5 min on one topic, long notes | One idea per slide, 45-90 s each, ask a question every 3-4 slides |

Rules for what stays:
- **Never show anything that looks like blame.** The slide "your child is the victim" idea becomes the whole frame: *you* are the target of a billion-dollar design team.
- **No death stories, suicide charts or method detail in front of 10-14s.** That cuts Sewell, Juliana, Adam, the Lost Screen Memorial, Kayleigh, and all four charts at the end. They are the strongest slides in the parent deck, but this age group needs safe-messaging rules (no stories of suicide, always a help route). Keep them in the parent deck. If a school or the family wants one AI case, use a non-fatal one with a counsellor present.
- **Always end a scary slide with "what to do".** The single most important sentence in the deck: *"If something online scares you, tell an adult you trust. You will not be in trouble."*
- Faith framing (the Quran verse, "haram", Islamic cartoons) is optional. It is kept in a few places as a swappable line, not built into the slides. **Decision needed from you**, see section 6.

## 4. Grabbing 10-14 year-olds

1. **Open with a bet, not a statistic.** "Raise your hand if you think you use your phone less than 3 hours a day." Then show 4.8.
2. **Position them as the smart ones being played.** "A team of 1,000 people is paid to make you scroll 10 more minutes. Let's see how it works." Teens hate being managed, love being *let in*.
3. **Show their world.** Replace Cocomelon/Ms. Rachel with Shorts/Reels/TikTok, Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite, Discord, Snapchat streaks, WhatsApp groups. Use screenshots of UI, not stock photos.
4. **Short, fast, one idea.** Max 8 words per headline, 20 s per builds, no paragraph slides. Existing animated slides (`gallup-teen-hours`, `hours-per-year`, `hours-tiles`, `game-dangers`) already fit.
5. **Two-minute interactions** (the deck's best feature is its builds, so use them for games): "Spot the trick", "Who's real? Susan or not?", "Spot the leaked info in this photo".
6. **Do it live.** The grayscale switch and the privacy toggles take 30 seconds each, and everyone's phone is in their pocket. If phones aren't allowed in the room, make them take-home cards.
7. **Voice:** speak like an older sibling, not a teacher. Say "they" about the companies, "you" for the audience. No "you must", use "you can".
8. **Humour is a tool.** A few jokes on the tricks (a "streak" that makes you open Snapchat at 11 pm for a flame emoji) win more than shock.
9. **Make the end a challenge, not a lecture:** 24 hours in grayscale; phone charging outside the bedroom for 7 nights; "post one fewer story".

## 5. What else to cover (not in the parent deck)

Ranked by value for 10-14s; the first four should be built, the rest are options.

1. **You're the product (how apps make money).** One slide: free app → your time → ads → "your attention is the price." 90 seconds, very effective.
2. **Cyberbullying: what to do** (1 in 2 in the DQ data). Don't reply, screenshot, block, tell someone, never post revenge. One slide, with a "being a bystander" line.
3. **Sextortion / "send me a pic" in a safe version.** Rule: the moment someone asks for a pic or moves you to a private app, stop and tell an adult. Show that the first move is a *compliment*, not a threat.
4. **Where to get help.** One slide with a trusted-adult list and local helplines (for the UK, Childline; confirm the number and region before using). Put it on the closing slide, big.
5. **Scams and fake accounts**: free Robux, fake giveaways, "verify your account". A 2-question quiz.
6. **In-game spending and loot boxes**: how a "chance" mechanic is designed like a slot machine.
7. **AI chatbots and AI-generated fakes (deepfake pics of classmates)**: the AI is not your friend, and sharing fakes of a classmate is serious.
8. **Digital footprint**: what you post stays; future schools and employers will see it.
9. **FOMO and comparison**: "everyone's highlights", filters, likes.
10. **Phones in the bedroom as a negotiated deal**: a "family agreement" template they can print.
